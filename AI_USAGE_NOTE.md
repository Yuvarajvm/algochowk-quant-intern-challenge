### 3. Human Decisions (Where I Guided the AI)
- **Falsification-First Mindset:** AI chatbots instinctively try to "make the strategy profitable" by fine-tuning thresholds or looking for complex indicators to generate high returns. I explicitly instructed the research framework to adopt an **unbiased, scientific falsification approach**—prioritizing truth and empirical reality over artificial backtest optimization.
- **Enforcing Observation Independence:** I identified that consecutive crash days (e.g., during the 2008 Lehman collapse or March 2020) violate the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption of standard hypothesis tests. I directed the creation of a configurable **lockout window** to evaluate non-overlapping events.
- **Unconditional Baseline Benchmarking:** AI initially evaluated post-event returns simply against zero ($H_0: \mu = 0$). I overrode this, pointing out that because equity indices possess positive natural drift (+0.044%/day in NIFTY), testing against zero is misleading. I forced all comparative tests to evaluate excess return against the **unconditional market baseline** over the exact same holding horizons.

---

### 4. Suggestions I Changed or Disagreed With
- **Threshold Overfitting:** The AI initially suggested scanning thresholds in 0.1% increments to find the "optimal" threshold. I rejected this because post-hoc parameter optimization causes severe **data snooping bias** (P-hacking). Instead, I constrained the analysis to pre-registered, economically intuitive thresholds (-1.0%, -1.5%, -2.0%, -2.5%, -3.0%).
- **Ignoring Overnight Gapping:** The AI assumed holding returns could simply be computed from $T$ Close to $T+h$ Close. I pointed out that entering at $T$ Close creates look-ahead bias unless a Market-On-Close (MOC) mechanism is explicitly modeled. I required a dual-model comparison ($T$ Close vs. $T+1$ Open) to expose the opening gap slippage.

---

### 5. Incorrect AI Suggestions Identified
1. **Look-Ahead Execution Bias:** An early script draft generated by the AI executed orders on day $T$ using day $T$'s Open price—an egregious temporal leak (buying at morning open based on an event confirmed only at afternoon close). I caught and corrected this immediately, ensuring signals at $T$ Close are strictly executed at $T+1$ Open.
2. **Exclusion of Statutory Indian Transaction Costs:** Standard AI backtesting templates omitted Indian market statutory friction (Securities Transaction Tax / STT, exchange turnover fees, stamp duty). I implemented an explicit 10 bps round-trip friction and 5 bps per leg slippage model, which revealed that nominal gross profits were completely wiped out.
3. **Overlapping Position Compounding:** The AI's initial backtesting loop accumulated leverage by stacking positions whenever multiple drops occurred back-to-back, artificially amplifying returns during recoveries and blowing up the account during crashes. I replaced this with an explicit single-position state-machine backtester.

---

### 6. Key Learnings & Takeaways
- **AI is an Accelerator, Not a Scientist:** AI is remarkably effective at generating bug-free vectorized syntax and statistical scaffolding, saving hours of manual coding. However, it lacks domain-grounded skepticism. Without strict human domain oversight, AI will readily optimize flawed strategies, introduce subtle look-ahead biases, and mistake statistical noise for alpha.
- **The Value of Falsification:** The most impactful quantitative research often lies in disproving intuitive market dogmas ("always buy the dip"). Rigorous falsification prevents deploying capital into structurally flawed strategies.
